01 / What I do

I find the problem behind the problem.

Most operational failures are not one broken device or one bad process. They happen where people, systems, vendors, and information stop lining up.

01.1

Operational visibility

Monitoring, inventory, incident history, and reporting that show what is happening before a customer has to say it first.

01.2

Systems integration

APIs, automations, and shared data views that connect products, teams, and companies that were never designed to work together.

01.3

Reliability and continuity

Redundancy, out-of-band management, recovery plans, and repair workflows that keep remote operations moving through failure.

01.4

Hands-on technical work

Tier 3 software support and deep hardware troubleshooting—from Linux and cloud infrastructure down to PCBs, backplanes, RF, and SDR systems.

02 / Selected outcomes

Proof over buzzwords.

The work is different in every environment. The pattern is consistent: make it visible, understand the failure, and leave the system stronger than I found it.

Distributed operations

Problems found before customers report them

Combined monitoring, inventory, alerting, repair history, and clearer reporting to replace reactive support with a usable view of statewide operations.

Business continuity

Weeks-long repairs reduced to roughly 24 hours

Introduced redundancy, hot-swappable replacements, centralized system images, and recovery thinking so workshop repairs no longer mean equivalent customer downtime.

Cloud efficiency

Infrastructure decisions tied to business runway

Contributed AWS cost reductions while maintaining prototype functionality—helping extend company operating runway by nine months.
